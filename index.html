Why no calcium chloride or table salt? Chloride pits stainless boilers — it's the #1 espresso machine killer, which is why La Marzocco caps it at 30 ppm. Magnesium sulfate delivers the same extraction hardness at 0 ppm chloride, and Mg doesn't form hard carbonate limescale the way Ca does. Chalk (CaCO₃) won't dissolve in still water anyway.

Chemistry: hardness as CaCO₃ = Epsom mg/L × 100.09/246.47 · alkalinity as CaCO₃ = NaHCO₃ mg/L × 50.04/84.01 · TDS meter estimate ≈ 0.7 × ion sum (conductivity meters under-read MgSO₄/NaHCO₃ vs NaCl calibration).

1 mL weighs 1 g, so a syringe or the coffee scale both work. The two solutions are in How to dose.

How to dose

  1. Mix two stock solutions, once. Epsom salt solution: 7.0 g Epsom salt in 1 L distilled water. Baking soda solution: 3.0 g baking soda in 1 L distilled water. Separate bottles.
  2. Measure the brew water into the kettle, carbon-filtered.
  3. Add each solution by syringe, or by weight with the kettle on the coffee scale.
  4. Swirl, then heat. Both salts are already dissolved.

Already have the espresso tab's concentrates? 100 mL of either, topped up to 1 L with distilled water, makes the same solution.

Carbon-filter the tap first. Seattle disinfects with free chlorine (0.4–1.0 mg/L at the tap), not chloramine, so plain carbon removes it. Pitcher filters with ion-exchange resin also pull hardness and alkalinity, which makes the tap numbers here read high.

Storage. Keep both capped and out of the sun. Remake one if it turns cloudy.

Comparing two waters. Small tests found minerals stirred into the brewed cup taste close to minerals in the brew water. Brew once on straight tap, split it, and dose one cup.

Why Epsom only for hardness? Tap already supplies the calcium (9 mg/L). Magnesium is what Seattle lacks, and blind tests disagree on which way magnesium versus calcium pushes flavor, so there's no evidence-backed reason to add a third salt. Sulfate lands far under its taste threshold.

Why a weaker solution than the espresso tab? One pourover needs about a tenth of a gallon, so the stock is a tenth the strength. The dose stays large enough to measure.

Target below tap? Salts only add. When a target is under the tap's hardness or alkalinity, the recipe cuts the tap with distilled first, then doses.
